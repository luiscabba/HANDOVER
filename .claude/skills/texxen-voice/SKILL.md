# texxen voice

One voice for everything texxen sends or publishes. It merges three sources: the texxen clamps, the no-ai-slop editing rules, and caveman lite with Simplified Technical English. Write in it first. Then run the check at the end.

## The clamps (from the texxen framework)

- Accountable, not proprietorial. Say what texxen will do and own. Never claim the client's work as ours.
- Plain, not blunt. Short words. No edge for its own sake.
- Understated, not withholding. Fewer claims, every one backed.
- Specific, not exhaustive. One concrete fact beats a list of five.

## Sentences (caveman lite + STE)

- One idea per sentence. Target 20 words or fewer.
- Active voice, a human or named organisation as subject. "SBC signs", not "sign-off is obtained".
- Present tense where true.
- Keep articles and full sentences. Cut filler, hedging and pleasantries.
- Short words: fix, not "implement a solution for"; use, not "utilise".
- One word, one meaning. Same term every time: gate is always gate, prototype is always prototype.
- Noun clusters of three words at most.
- Never drop not, never, no, only or except.
- Numbers, dates and units exact. Dates as "13 November 2026", amounts as "PHP 11,000".

## Cut on sight (no-ai-slop)

- Words: delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, cutting-edge, seamless, holistic, synergy, transformative, elevate, unlock, harness, journey (unless a real user journey), ecosystem, world-class, best-in-class, end-to-end (unless literal).
- Empty adverbs: just, really, simply, actually, truly, fundamentally, crucially.
- Empty phrases: it's worth noting, at its core, when it comes to, in today's world, in order to, going forward, the reality is.
- Binary contrasts: "not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y". State Y.
- Negative lists: "Not a vendor. Not an agency. A partner." Say what it is.
- Colon reveals: "The best part: it learns." Write a plain sentence. Colons are for lists and labels.
- Throat-clearing and faux insight: "here's the thing", "what most people miss".
- Importance puffery: "a pivotal moment", "plays a vital role", "a testament to". State the fact.
- Trailing -ing analysis: "..., highlighting our commitment to". Cut or replace with a consequence.
- Fake-profound kickers and triple mic-drops at the end of a page. Delete. End on the last concrete point or the next action.
- Weasel attribution: "experts agree", "studies show". Name the source or cut.
- Em dashes. None.

## Proof over adjectives

If a sentence could sit unchanged in another firm's proposal, cut it or add a fact only texxen has: a number, a named system, a date, a mechanism. "We deliver results" fails. "OpenCI tracks 835 field agents live" passes.

Never invent the fact. Unknown values stay bracketed: [DATE], [n], [name].

## Page rules

- Lead with the claim or the decision. Reasoning after.
- Headlines are statements, not puns: "Six house rules", not "Rules that keep a gate a gate".
- A page that needs more than three short paragraphs is two pages or a diagram.
- Name the organisation, never "the client" in client-facing copy.
- State exclusions plainly, without apology.

## Check before it ships

1. Any banned word, empty adverb or empty phrase left?
2. Any binary contrast, negative list, colon reveal or kicker?
3. Any sentence over 25 words, or with two ideas?
4. Any passive voice that hides who acts?
5. Any sentence that passes the portability test into another firm's document?
6. Any invented number or fact?
7. Any em dash?

Fix every yes, then read the page aloud once. If it sounds like a person at texxen talking to SBC, it ships.
